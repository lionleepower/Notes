# Listening Note: Connected Speech Practice

## Source Lines

```text
Mike.
Make sure to keep me in the loop about any updates.
```

## Whole Flow Marking

```text
Mike.
→ Mi[k̚]e.

Make sure to keep me in the loop about any updates.
→ Ma[k̚]e_sure tə kee[p̚]_me-(j)-in_the loo[p]_abou[ɾ]_any [j]pdates.
```

## Main Listening Focus

The sentence is spoken as a continuous flow rather than as separate words.

```text
Make sure | to keep me in the loop | about any updates.
→ Ma[k̚]e_sure | tə kee[p̚]_me-(j)-in_the_loop | abou[ɾ]_any u[p̚]dates.
```

The most important phrase is:

```text
keep me in the loop
```

Meaning: keep me informed and tell me about any new developments.

## Pronunciation Pattern 1: Held Final Stops

A final stop consonant is often not fully released when another consonant follows it.

```text
Mike
→ Mi[k̚]e

make sure
→ ma[k̚]e_sure

keep me
→ kee[p̚]_me

updates
→ u[p̚]dates
```

Listening approximations:

```text
make sure
→ mayk-sure
The /k/ is present, but there is no strong release.

keep me
→ keep-me
The /p/ is closed quickly before /m/.
```

## Pronunciation Pattern 2: Linking, Weak “to,” and Flap T

Words connect naturally when the first word ends with a consonant and the next word begins with a vowel.

```text
loop about
→ loo[p]_about
```

The word `to` is usually reduced in unstressed speech.

```text
to keep
→ tə keep
```

In American English, the final `t` in `about` commonly becomes a Flap T before the vowel sound in `any`.

```text
about any
→ abou[ɾ]_any
```

Listening approximation:

```text
about any
→ aboud-any
```

A light `/j/` glide may appear between the two adjacent vowel sounds in `me in`.

```text
me in
→ me-(j)-in
```

## Core Takeaways

- Do not pronounce `to` as a strong `/tuː/` sound in this sentence. Use `tə`.
    
- Hold the final `/k/` in `make` before `sure`.
    
- Hold the final `/p/` in `keep` before `me`.
    
- Link `loop_about` smoothly without inserting a pause.
    
- Pronounce `about_any` with a Flap T: `abou[ɾ]_any`.
    
- Practise `keep me in the loop` as one complete speech unit.
    

## Common Mistakes

- Pronouncing every word separately:
    

```text
Make | sure | to | keep | me | in | the | loop | about | any | updates.
```

- Using an overly strong pronunciation of `to`:
    

```text
to keep
→ /tuː kiːp/
```

- Releasing the `/k/` and `/p/` too strongly:
    

```text
make-K sure
keep-P me
```

- Pronouncing `about any` with a fully released `/t/`:
    

```text
about-T any
```
