# Listening Note: Connected Speech Practice

## Source Lines

```text
He kept his personal life at arm's length from colleagues.
```

## Whole Flow Marking

```text
He kept his personal life at arm's length from colleagues.
→ He kep[t̚]_his personal life_a[ɾ]_arm's_length | frəm_c[a]lleagues.
```

Possible listening approximation:

```text
He kep-tiz personal life uh-darm's length | frum colleagues.
```

## Main Listening Focus

The main challenge is recognizing the phrase **keep someone or something at arm's length** when several words are compressed into one continuous sound group.

```text
at arm's length
→ a[ɾ]_arm's_length
```

Meaning:

```text
keep something at arm's length
→ deliberately avoid becoming too close to it
→ 保持距离，不让某件事过多进入自己的私人生活
```

In this sentence:

```text
He kept his personal life at arm's length from colleagues.
```

means that he did not share much about his private life with his colleagues.

## Pronunciation Pattern 1: Weak Form and Flap T in "at arm's"

In natural American English, **at** is usually unstressed in this phrase. Its vowel becomes weaker, and the final **t** may sound like a quick flap `[ɾ]` before the vowel in **arm's**.

```text
at arm's
→ a[ɾ]_arm's
```

Listening approximation:

```text
at arm's
→ uh-darm's
```

Do not expect a carefully separated pronunciation:

```text
at | arm's
```

## Pronunciation Pattern 2: Held T in "kept his"

The final **t** in **kept** is often not fully released before the next consonant sound. The word may sound shorter and less distinct than its spelling suggests.

```text
kept his
→ kep[t̚]_his
```

In faster speech, the **h** in **his** may also become weak, so the phrase can sound close to:

```text
kept his
→ kep-tiz
```

## Pronunciation Pattern 3: Reduced "from"

The word **from** is not the focus of the sentence, so its vowel is commonly reduced to a schwa sound.

```text
from colleagues
→ frəm_colleagues
```

Listening approximation:

```text
from colleagues
→ frum colleagues
```

## Pronunciation Pattern 4: The Final Sound in "length"

The word **length** ends with the consonant cluster `/ŋθ/`. Avoid inserting a strong `/g/` sound.

```text
length
→ /leŋθ/
```

Compare:

```text
length
→ /leŋθ/

egg
→ /eɡ/
```

The ending of **length** should not sound like:

```text
leng-gth
```

## Core Takeaways

- Treat the phrase as one sound group:
    

```text
at arm's length
→ a[ɾ]_arm's_length
```

- The word **at** is weak and compressed in natural speech.
    
- The final **t** in **kept** is usually not strongly released:
    

```text
kep[t̚]_his
```

- The word **from** is often reduced:
    

```text
from
→ frəm
```

- Pay attention to the `/ŋθ/` ending in:
    

```text
length
```

- The full sentence should flow in two main chunks:
    

```text
He kep[t̚]_his personal life_a[ɾ]_arm's_length | frəm_colleagues.
```

## Common Mistakes

- Pronouncing every word separately:
    

```text
at | arm's | length
```

- Using a strong, fully released **t** in:
    

```text
kept his
```

- Pronouncing **from** too carefully as a fully stressed word.
    
- Adding an unnecessary `/g/` sound inside:
    

```text
length
```

### Phrase Meaning

What does this phrase mean?

```text
keep something at arm's length
```

Answer:

```text
to deliberately keep some distance from something
```

### Pronunciation Recognition

Which version is closer to natural American speech?

```text
A. at | arm's | length

```

Answer:

```text
B. a[ɾ]_arm's_length
```

What happens to the final **t** in this phrase?

```text
kept his
```

Answer:

```text
It is usually held and not strongly released:
kep[t̚]_his
```
