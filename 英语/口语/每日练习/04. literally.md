## Main Listening Focus

The most important feature in this clip is that several short words are attached to the words around them. Do not listen for each word separately.

The stressed words carry the rhythm:

```text
I'm GETting BACK into the GROOVE of THINGS.

Don't WORry about it.

Were you EVer in the GROOVE?
```

==Useful phrases:==

```text
get back into the groove of things
→ return to a familiar routine after a break
→ 重新进入状态，恢复原来的节奏

be in the groove
→ feel comfortable, focused, and fully engaged in an activity
→ 进入状态

calisthenics
→ exercises that mainly use your own body weight, such as push-ups or squats
```

## Pronunciation Pattern 1: Flap T

In American English, a `t` between vowel sounds often becomes a quick tap `[ɾ]`. It may sound closer to a soft `d`.

```text
getting
→ ge[ɾ]ing

Listening approximation:
→ "gedding"
```

```text
about it
→ abou[ɾ]_it

Listening approximation:
→ "abou-dit"
```

The word boundary becomes difficult to notice:

```text
Don't worry about it.
→ Don'[t̚] worry_abou[ɾ]_it.
```

## Pronunciation Pattern 2: Linking and Boundary Smoothing

When one word ends with a consonant and the next word starts with a vowel, the two words are often pronounced as one continuous unit.

```text
doing some
→ doing_some
```

```text
back into
→ back_into
```

```text
groove of
→ groove_əv
```

```text
worry about
→ worry_about
```

```text
ever in
→ ever_in
```

The word `of` is weak and reduced:

```text
groove of things
→ groove_əv things
```

A small `/w/` glide may appear between `you` and `ever` because the two vowel sounds meet:

```text
you ever
→ you-(w)-ever
```

The final `t` in `don't` is usually not strongly released before `worry`:

```text
Don't worry
→ Don'[t̚] worry
```

## Core Takeaways

- Do not separate `back` and `into`; listen for one continuous unit: `back_into`.
    
- The `t` in `getting` is a flap `[ɾ]`, so it may sound like `gedding`.
    
- The phrase `about it` often sounds closer to `abou-dit`.
    
- The word `of` is weak in `groove of things`: `groove_əv things`.
    
- The final `t` in `don't` is not strongly released before `worry`.
    
- A light `/w/` glide may connect `you` and `ever`: `you-(w)-ever`.
    

## Common Mistakes

- Do not pronounce the `t` in `getting` as a strong, isolated `/t/`.
    
- Do not pause between `back` and `into`.
    
- Do not stress `of` in `the groove of things`.
    
- Do not force a clearly released `t` at the end of `don't`.
    
- Do not miss the idiomatic meaning of `get back into the groove of things`; it is not literally about music.
